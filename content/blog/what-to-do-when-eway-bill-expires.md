An e-way bill has a fixed life. Once it expires, goods still on the road are treated as moving without a valid e-way bill, and that can lead to detention, seizure and penalty under Section 129 of the CGST Act. The good news is that the rules give a defined window to extend validity when genuine delays happen. The bad news is that the window is short and easy to miss.

## First, know exactly when your e-way bill expires

Under Rule 138(10) of the CGST Rules, 2017, validity depends on distance:

| Type of cargo | Validity |
|---|---|
| Normal cargo | 1 day for every 200 km or part thereof |
| Over-dimensional cargo (ODC) | 1 day for every 20 km or part thereof |

The period is counted from the time of generation, and the first "day" ends at midnight of the day following generation. So an e-way bill generated at 11:00 pm on 5 August with 1 day of validity is valid till midnight of 6 August — not just one hour.

The validity end time is printed on the e-way bill itself. Transporters should treat that timestamp as a hard deadline. For details on how distance drives validity, see [e-way bill validity explained](/blog/eway-bill-validity-explained).

## The extension window

Where goods cannot reach the destination within validity because of exceptional circumstances — for example an accident, natural calamity, law and order problems or trans-shipment delays — the generator or the transporter in charge can extend validity **within 8 hours before or 8 hours after expiry**.

```
          8 hrs before          expiry          8 hrs after
   -----------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------
              [========= extension allowed ==========]
```

Outside this 16-hour band, the portal will not allow extension.

There is also an overall cap. As reported in GSTN advisories, from 1 January 2025 an e-way bill can be extended only up to a total of **360 days from its original generation date**. This mainly affects goods stuck for long periods, but it is worth knowing.

## How extension works on the portal

Broadly, the steps are:

1. Log in to the e-way bill portal (or the E-Way Bill 2.0 portal).
2. Choose the **extend validity** option and enter the e-way bill number.
3. Confirm you want to extend and select the **reason** (accident, natural calamity, law and order, trans-shipment, others).
4. Enter the **current location** of the goods — place and PIN code — and the **remaining distance** to the destination.
5. Update the **vehicle number** or transport details if they have changed.
6. Submit. The portal calculates fresh validity based on the remaining distance you entered.

The important point is that the new validity is based on the **remaining distance from the current location**, not the original full distance.

## Example (illustrative): flood delay on a Pune–Kolkata route

A Pune auto-components manufacturer ships goods worth ₹6,80,000 to Kolkata. Distance is roughly 1,900 km.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Generated | 1 July, 2:00 pm |
| Validity | 1,900 ÷ 200 = 9.5 → 10 days |
| Expiry | Midnight of 11 July |
| Extension window | 4:00 pm on 11 July to 8:00 am on 12 July |

Heavy rain closes a highway stretch in Odisha and the truck is held up near Cuttack for four days. On 11 July evening, the truck is still about 450 km from Kolkata.

At 6:30 pm on 11 July — inside the window — the transporter extends the e-way bill:

- Reason: natural calamity
- Current place: near Cuttack, with the PIN code of that location
- Remaining distance: 450 km
- New validity: 450 ÷ 200 = 2.25 → 3 days

The goods now have fresh validity counted from the extension. The transporter keeps a note of the road closure (news report, transporter's message log) in case an officer asks during transit.

If the transporter had remembered only at 10:00 am on 12 July, the window would have closed two hours earlier.

## If the e-way bill has already expired and the window is gone

This is the situation to avoid, because the options are limited:

- **Do not move the goods further on the expired e-way bill.** Every kilometre travelled without a valid e-way bill adds to the risk.
- **Check the portal for your options.** The portal generally does not allow a second e-way bill for the same document by the same generator while the first stands, and documents older than 180 days cannot be used for fresh generation (reported by GSTN from 1 January 2025). What is possible depends on your facts.
- **Speak to your tax consultant** before taking any step, especially if the goods are of high value or have already been stopped.
- **Document the reason for delay** — repair bills, police reports, weather advisories. These do not make an expired e-way bill valid, but they help explain the facts if proceedings start.

## Build habits that prevent expiry

- **Set an alert** a few hours before expiry for every long-distance consignment. A shared sheet with e-way bill number, expiry time and driver contact works for small fleets.
- **Ask drivers to report delays early**, not at the destination.
- **Enter realistic distance.** The portal auto-calculates PIN-to-PIN distance and allows up to 10% more. Entering too little distance gives you less validity than the route actually needs. Our [distance calculator](/tools/distance-calculator) gives a quick estimate.
- **Generate the e-way bill close to dispatch**, not the day before. Validity starts at generation, not when the truck leaves.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming validity starts when the truck departs.
- Trying to extend 10 hours after expiry.
- Entering the original full distance as remaining distance, or an unrealistically low figure.
- Confusing a Part B vehicle update with an extension — a vehicle change does not add any time.
- Forgetting that over-dimensional cargo has a much shorter validity per kilometre.
