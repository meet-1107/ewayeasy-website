> **Quick answer:** Movement within a state follows the same e-way bill framework, but the threshold is not always ₹50,000, because some states have notified different limits or exemptions. Check your state's current notification. Part B is not needed for same-state movement up to 50 km to or from a transporter; Part A still is.

Intra-state movement looks easy: the truck stays in the state, trips are short and the tax is CGST + SGST. Yet it is where many businesses get caught, because the rules are not identical across India. A practice that works in one state may be wrong in the next.

## What changes when goods stay within one state

Intra-state movement uses the same law everywhere: Section 68 of the CGST Act and Rule 138 of the CGST Rules. What can change is the state layer: a state may notify its own value threshold, intra-city relief or goods-specific rules for movement inside it. So the national rule is your starting point, and your state's notification is the final word.

The national framework gives you Form GST EWB-01 with Part A and Part B, three possible generators (supplier, recipient or transporter), and the Annexure exemptions. The tax on the invoice is CGST + SGST, not IGST.

The state layer is what this article is about. Inter-state loads ignore it completely; for those, see [inter-state e-way bill requirements](/blog/eway-bill-interstate-movement-requirements).

## Thresholds and relaxations that can differ by state

Rule 138 lets states set conditions for movement inside their borders. In practice, four things can differ: the value threshold, relief for movement within a city, rules for specified goods, and how value is assessed. This site does not list state-wise figures, because notifications change and a stale number is worse than none.

| What may differ | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Value threshold | Some states have notified a different (higher) threshold for intra-state movement than the general ₹50,000 |
| Intra-city movement | Some states have relaxed the requirement for movement within a city or municipal limits |
| Specified goods | Some states require e-way bills for certain goods regardless of the general limit, or exempt others |
| How value is assessed | States may specify whether the limit applies per invoice or to the whole consignment in a vehicle |

To find your state's position, check the notifications on your state's commercial tax department website and the information on the official e-way bill portal. Note the notification reference, not just the number, so you can re-check it later.

Three decision rules keep this safe:

- **If** you cannot find your state's current notification, **then** apply the general ₹50,000 rule.
- **If** a load is near the limit, **then** generate one. Voluntary generation is allowed, while moving without one when it was required can lead to detention and penalty under Section 129.
- **If** a supplier from another state tells you "we don't need it here", **then** check your own state's rule. Theirs does not apply to you.

## The 50 km Part B relief

The 50 km relief means Part B need not be filled when goods move within the same state up to 50 km from your place to a transporter's place for onward transport, or from the transporter's place to the consignee. Part A is still generated, so this is relief for short transporter legs, not a general exemption.

| If the trip is… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Your factory → transporter's godown, same state, up to 50 km | Part A only for this leg; the transporter fills Part B before the long leg |
| Transporter's godown → consignee, same state, up to 50 km | Part A covers it; Part B not required for this leg |
| Your factory → buyer directly, 30 km, your own vehicle | Normal rule: fill both parts |
| Any distance, crossing a state border | Relief does not apply |

The misreading to avoid is "no e-way bill under 50 km". The relief is about Part B, and only for movement to or from a transporter. A direct local delivery in your own tempo needs both parts if the value crosses your state's limit.

## Short trips and the one-day validity trap

Most intra-state trips are under 200 km, so the e-way bill gets one day of validity. That "day" ends at midnight of the day after validity starts, not 24 hours later. So the clock matters: an e-way bill made late in the evening gives the driver barely more than a day, and one made too early can expire before loading.

Generate close to the actual dispatch time, not the night before "to save time". If the truck is late the next morning, validity is already ticking. If it then misses the window, you need an extension, which the rule allows only for exceptional circumstances.

Two portal points help on local routes:

- **Same PIN code.** Where dispatch and delivery PINs are the same, the portal lets you enter a distance of up to 100 km. Enter a realistic figure.
- **Validity starts with Part B.** Validity is counted from when Part B is first entered. Under the 50 km relief, the clock starts when the transporter fills Part B for the main leg.

## Stock transfers and job work within the state

A transfer to your own godown under the same GSTIN is not a supply, so it moves on a delivery challan. The e-way bill rules still apply if the value crosses your state's threshold. Intra-state job work follows the same threshold; only inter-state job work needs an e-way bill at any value.

| Movement within the state | Document | E-way bill |
|---|---|---|
| To your godown, same GSTIN | Delivery challan | If value crosses your state's limit; reason such as own use |
| To your own separate registration in the same state | Tax invoice (distinct persons) | If value crosses your state's limit |
| To a job worker | Delivery challan | If value crosses your state's limit |

The separate-registration row surprises people. A second GSTIN in the same state, such as a separate business vertical, is a distinct person, so a challan alone is usually not enough. See [delivery challan vs tax invoice](/blog/delivery-challan-vs-tax-invoice).

For job work, the "irrespective of value" rule applies only across states. Within the state, your normal limit applies; see [e-way bill rules for job work](/blog/eway-bill-rules-for-job-work).

## Example (illustrative): one day, three dispatches in Tamil Nadu

This example shows how one business applies the state layer on a normal day. A dispatch above the limit gets a full e-way bill after checking the state notification, and a small one may need none. A long trip through a transporter uses the 50 km relief for the first leg only.

A Coimbatore pump manufacturer (GSTIN `33ABCDE1234F1Z5`) has three dispatches, all within Tamil Nadu:

1. **Pumps worth ₹85,000 incl. GST to a dealer 40 km away, by own vehicle.** This is above the general limit. The accounts team checks the current state notification first, then generates an e-way bill with Part A and Part B. The 50 km relief does not apply, because no transporter is involved.
2. **Spare parts worth ₹30,000 to a local mechanic.** Below the general threshold, so no e-way bill unless the state has a lower or goods-specific rule.
3. **Pumps worth ₹3,20,000 to a buyer 300 km away, via a transporter.** The goods go by tempo to the transporter's godown 8 km away with Part A only. The transporter fills Part B with the truck number before the long leg, giving 2 days of validity.

## Common mistakes

Intra-state mistakes mostly come from borrowed rules and loose timing. Teams apply another state's practice, read the 50 km relief as a blanket exemption, or skip e-way bills for branch transfers because no sale happened. Others generate too early, so a one-day e-way bill expires before the goods move.

- **Assuming another state's rule applies to you.** A supplier's practice in a different state is not a guide to yours.
- **Treating "under 50 km" as a blanket exemption.** The relief covers Part B for transporter legs only.
- **IGST on an intra-state invoice.** Intra-state supplies attract CGST + SGST; see [CGST, SGST and IGST explained](/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-explained).
- **No e-way bill for branch transfers.** Not being a sale does not take the movement outside the rule.
- **Relying on old notifications.** Re-check at least at the start of each financial year.
- **Generating too early.** A one-day e-way bill made the evening before a delayed dispatch can expire first.

## Quick action checklist

1. Find your state's current e-way bill notification and record the threshold, any intra-city rule and the reference in your dispatch SOP.
2. Set a calendar reminder to re-check it at the start of each financial year.
3. Tell the dispatch desk the exact test for the 50 km relief: same state, to or from a transporter, Part A still needed.
4. Generate e-way bills close to the actual dispatch time, never the night before.
5. List your godowns and registrations in the state, and mark which transfers need a challan and which need an invoice.
