> **Quick answer:** Rule 138 covers movement of goods, not only sales, so returns, approval supplies, exhibition goods and similar cases can need an e-way bill once the threshold is crossed. Each physical movement needs its own e-way bill. Where no sale takes place, the document is usually a delivery challan.

Most e-way bill guidance assumes a simple sale from seller to buyer. Real business is messier: goods come back, go out on approval, travel to fairs, or get billed to one party and delivered to another. The rules still apply, but the document, the reason and the generator can change.

This is general guidance, not a legal opinion. The facts of each arrangement matter, so verify the current position on the official portal or with your adviser.

## The principle that covers all of these

Rule 138 applies to the movement of goods, whether in relation to a supply, for reasons other than supply, or due to inward supply from an unregistered person. So the question is never just "was there a sale?". You ask whether goods are moving, whether the threshold is crossed, and whether an exemption applies.

Run every special case through three questions:

1. Are goods physically moving?
2. Does the consignment value cross the applicable threshold, or is it a case needing an e-way bill at any value?
3. Are the goods or the situation exempt under the Annexure to Rule 138(14)?

Where goods move without a sale, the document is usually a **delivery challan** under Rule 55. The e-way bill is generated against it with the matching reason. See [delivery challan vs tax invoice](/blog/delivery-challan-vs-tax-invoice) for how to choose.

## 1. Sales returns

A sales return is a new movement in the reverse direction, from buyer back to supplier. It needs its own e-way bill if the value of returned goods crosses the threshold. The outward e-way bill cannot be reused, and whoever causes the return movement generates it, commonly the registered buyer, the transporter, or the supplier as recipient.

- **Who generates:** whoever causes the movement. That is commonly the buyer (if registered) or the transporter; the original supplier can also generate it as the recipient.
- **Document:** practice varies. Many businesses use a delivery challan or the buyer's own document for the return; the supplier issues a credit note in its books. Pick one practice and confirm it with your adviser.
- **Reason:** the portal provides a sales return option.
- **Value:** the value of the goods actually coming back, including tax.

Two points get mixed up here. Rejecting an e-way bill on the portal within 72 hours is a response to the record; it does not move goods back. And a partial return is valued on what comes back, not on the original invoice total.

## 2. Supply on approval

Supply on approval means goods go to a customer who may accept or reject them, as is common in jewellery, machinery and textiles. The sale is not complete when goods leave, so they move on a delivery challan. If the customer accepts, a tax invoice follows within the time limit in the law.

- **Document:** delivery challan at the time of sending; a tax invoice when the customer accepts.
- **E-way bill:** if the value crosses the threshold, generate one against the challan.
- **If goods come back:** the return movement may need its own e-way bill.

Partial acceptance is the case to plan for. If the customer keeps half, you invoice that half and the rest returns as its own movement. Keep a register of approval challans so nothing stays open past the time limit.

## 3. Exhibitions and fairs

Goods sent to a trade fair are moved for a reason other than supply, so they travel on a delivery challan. If the value crosses the threshold, an e-way bill is generally needed, with the exhibition or fairs reason. Unsold goods coming back are a second movement, checked again on the value actually returning.

- **Document:** delivery challan.
- **Reason:** the portal has an exhibition or fairs option.
- **Unsold goods coming back:** a fresh movement; check whether an e-way bill is needed.
- **Inter-state exhibitions:** these can raise questions about registration and tax at the venue, so get advice before you go.

Plan the return leg before the fair ends. Stands are packed in a hurry, and the return truck often leaves before anyone has worked out what was sold. Count the stock, raise the return challan, then generate.

## 4. Bill-to-ship-to and bill-from-dispatch-from

In a bill-to-ship-to deal, A sells to B, but on B's instruction the goods go straight from A to C. In bill-from-dispatch-from, goods start from a place other than the supplier's own, such as a third party's warehouse. There is one physical movement, so one e-way bill is needed, with the actual dispatch and delivery points.

The portal provides transaction type options for these deals, alongside the regular type and a combination type. In general:

- Only **one e-way bill** is needed for the single physical movement.
- Part A should show the **actual dispatch and delivery locations**, because those decide distance and validity.
- The billing parties' GSTINs go in the portal's fields for that transaction type.

The tax type is a separate question from the route. In a bill-to-ship-to deal, the place of supply can be in a different state from the delivery point; see [CGST, SGST and IGST explained](/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-explained). Read the [user documentation](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/) before your first one, because the field mapping is specific.

## 5. Line sales

Line sales means a van leaves the godown with stock and sells to shops along a route, without knowing in advance who will buy what. The outgoing load moves on a delivery challan, and tax invoices are raised as each sale is made. The supplier generates the e-way bill for the load if its value crosses the threshold.

- **Document:** delivery challan for the goods leaving the godown; tax invoices as each sale is made.
- **Reason:** the portal provides line sales and recipient-not-known options for such movements.
- **E-way bill:** generated by the supplier for the outgoing load if the value crosses the threshold.

Value the challan on the whole load, not the sales you expect. The van carries the whole load from the moment it leaves, and that is what an officer will see.

## 6. Goods sent in SKD/CKD condition or in lots

Large machinery often goes in semi-knocked-down (SKD) or completely-knocked-down (CKD) condition, over several vehicles and days. Rule 55 sets the method: issue the complete invoice before the first lot, move each lot on a delivery challan referring to that invoice, and generate a separate e-way bill for each lot with that lot's value.

The general approach under Rule 55:

- The supplier issues the **complete invoice before the first consignment**.
- Each consignment moves with a **delivery challan referring to the invoice**.
- Each consignment carries its challan with a **duly certified copy of the invoice**, and the **original invoice goes with the last consignment**. Check the current rule text.
- An **e-way bill is generated for each consignment** against its challan, with the value of that lot. The portal has an SKD/CKD/lots option.

The lot values should add up to the invoice. Keep a small table of lot, challan number, value and e-way bill number, so the totals reconcile when the last lot leaves.

## Summary table

This table sums up the six special cases. For each one, it shows the usual document, the typical reason option on the portal, and who often generates. Treat it as a first reference: option names on the portal can change between versions, so pick the one that best describes the actual movement.

| Situation | Usual document | Typical reason option | Who often generates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales return | Delivery challan or as per practice | Sales return | Buyer (if registered) / transporter |
| Supply on approval | Delivery challan | As per portal options | Supplier |
| Exhibition / fair | Delivery challan | Exhibition or fairs | Supplier / owner of goods |
| Bill-to-ship-to | Tax invoice | Supply (with bill-to-ship-to transaction type) | Supplier or as agreed |
| Line sales | Delivery challan | Line sales / recipient not known | Supplier |
| SKD/CKD in lots | Invoice + delivery challan per lot | SKD/CKD/lots | Supplier |

Who generates in each case is covered in more depth in [who is responsible for generating an e-way bill](/blog/who-is-responsible-for-generating-eway-bill).

## Example (illustrative): a machine in three lots, then a return

This example combines two special cases: a machine goes to the buyer in three lots, each on its own challan and e-way bill under one invoice. A month later, a faulty part comes back. That return is a fresh movement with its own e-way bill and a credit note in the supplier's books.

A Pune machine builder (GSTIN `27ABCDE1234F1Z5`) sells a packaging machine worth ₹18,00,000 plus GST to a buyer in Nashik. It is too large for one truck, so it goes in three lots on three days.

1. Before the first lot, the builder issues the full tax invoice `PMB/26-27/0042`.
2. Each lot moves with a delivery challan referring to that invoice: `DC/26-27/0301`, `0302`, `0303`.
3. For each lot, an e-way bill is generated against its challan, with that lot's value, the SKD/CKD/lots option and the truck number in Part B.
4. Each lot carries a certified copy of the invoice, and the original travels with the last lot.

A month later, the buyer returns a defective control panel worth ₹1,20,000 incl. GST. The buyer, being registered, generates an e-way bill for the return with the sales return option. The builder issues a credit note in its books.

## Common mistakes

Special-case mistakes usually come from treating every movement like a normal sale. People assume no sale means no e-way bill, reuse the outward e-way bill for a return, or put all SKD lots on one e-way bill. Others pick the billing PIN in bill-to-ship-to deals, or select "Supply" for everything.

- **Assuming no sale means no e-way bill.** Movement for reasons other than supply is covered.
- **Reusing an old e-way bill for the return trip.**
- **One e-way bill for all SKD/CKD lots.** Each vehicle and lot needs its own.
- **Wrong delivery PIN in bill-to-ship-to cases.** The billing address PIN gives the wrong distance.
- **Selecting "Supply" for everything.** The reason should match the actual movement.
- **No delivery challan for line sales or exhibitions.** Goods on the road need a document.

## Quick action checklist

1. List the special movements your business actually makes, such as returns, approval, fairs or lots.
2. For each one, write down the document, the portal reason and who generates, using the summary table.
3. Keep a register of open approval and exhibition challans with a due date.
4. For SKD/CKD orders, keep a lot table that reconciles to the invoice total.
5. Read the official user documentation once before your first bill-to-ship-to e-way bill.
