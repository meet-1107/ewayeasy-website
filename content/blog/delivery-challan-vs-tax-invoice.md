Goods leave your premises for many reasons. Sometimes you are selling them. Sometimes you are sending them to a job worker for processing, to a customer on approval, to your own branch, or out with a sales van that will sell along the route. GST treats these differently, and the document you issue should reflect what is actually happening.

The rule of thumb: **a tax invoice is for a supply; a delivery challan is for movement that is not (yet) a supply, or where the invoice cannot be issued at the time of removal.**

## The legal basis in brief

- **Tax invoice:** Section 31 of the CGST Act and Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017.
- **Delivery challan:** Rule 55 of the CGST Rules, 2017.

Rule 55 allows a delivery challan to be issued, instead of an invoice, at the time of removal of goods in specified cases. The main ones are:

- Supply of liquid gas where the quantity at the time of removal is not known
- Transportation of goods for job work
- Transportation of goods for reasons other than by way of supply
- Other supplies as may be notified (such as goods sent on approval)

The rule also covers goods moved in semi-knocked down (SKD) or completely knocked down (CKD) condition in several lots, where the full invoice is issued before the first consignment and each lot moves with a delivery challan.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Point | Tax invoice | Delivery challan |
|---|---|---|
| Used when | A taxable supply of goods takes place | Goods move without a sale, or the sale is not complete at removal |
| Tax charged? | Yes, shown on the document | No tax liability arises from the challan itself |
| Basis for ITC | Yes, for the recipient | No |
| Typical cases | Normal sales, inter-state sales, exports | Job work, approval sales, line sales, stock transfers within the same GSTIN, exhibitions, SKD/CKD lots |
| Copies (goods) | Original — recipient; duplicate — transporter; triplicate — supplier | Original — consignee; duplicate — transporter; triplicate — consignor |
| Serial number | Consecutive series, unique for the financial year | Consecutive series, unique for the financial year |
| E-way bill basis? | Yes | Yes |

> **Note:** A stock transfer to a branch in **another state** is generally treated as a supply between distinct persons (different GSTINs), so it usually needs a tax invoice, not just a challan. Within the same state and same GSTIN, a delivery challan is typical. Confirm the treatment for your situation.

## What a delivery challan should contain

Rule 55 lists the particulars. In practical terms, your challan should have:

- Date and number (consecutive, unique series not exceeding 16 characters)
- Name, address and GSTIN of the consignor
- Name, address and GSTIN (or UIN) of the consignee, if registered
- HSN code and description of goods
- Quantity (provisional where exact quantity is not known)
- Taxable value
- Tax rate and tax amount — where the transportation is for supply to the consignee
- Place of supply, in the case of inter-state movement
- Signature

Many businesses print "Not a tax invoice" or the purpose (for example, "Sent for job work") on the challan to avoid confusion at the receiving end.

## Example (illustrative): job work and final sale

A Ludhiana hosiery unit sends 2,000 kg of yarn to a job worker in Tiruppur for knitting. Later, it sells the finished fabric to a garment exporter in Mumbai.

**Movement 1 — yarn to the job worker (Punjab → Tamil Nadu)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Document | Delivery challan DC/26/041 |
| Purpose | Sent for job work |
| Value of yarn | ₹4,60,000 |
| Tax on document | Not charged as a supply |
| E-way bill | Required — inter-state movement from principal to job worker needs one irrespective of value |

**Movement 2 — fabric back from the job worker**

The job worker returns the knitted fabric to Ludhiana. This return movement is also covered by a challan, and an e-way bill is needed where the conditions apply. Our article on [e-way bill rules for job work](/blog/eway-bill-rules-for-job-work) covers who generates it.

**Movement 3 — sale to the Mumbai exporter (Punjab → Maharashtra)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fabric, 1,750 kg × ₹380 | ₹6,65,000 |
| IGST @ 5% (illustrative rate) | ₹33,250 |
| **Invoice total** | **₹6,98,250** |

This is a supply, so a tax invoice is issued with IGST (inter-state), and an e-way bill is generated against that invoice.

Three movements, two document types, three e-way bills — and each document matches what was actually happening at the time.

## Supply on approval and line sales

**Goods sent on approval:** When a jeweller or machinery dealer sends goods to a customer to inspect before buying, the sale is not complete at removal. A delivery challan covers the movement. If the customer accepts, a tax invoice is issued. GST law sets a time limit for issuing the invoice in such cases, so check the current provision.

**Line sales / sales van:** Where the quantity to be sold to each customer is not known at dispatch, goods can move under a delivery challan and invoices are raised as each sale is made on the route.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a challan for an actual sale** to delay tax. If ownership passes and consideration is agreed, it is a supply and needs an invoice.
- **Using an invoice for job work.** Sending goods to a job worker is not a sale; an invoice charging GST here confuses both parties' books.
- **Forgetting the e-way bill for job work.** Inter-state movement to a job worker needs an e-way bill irrespective of value.
- **Not converting approval challans into invoices** within the time limit.
- **Mixing challan and invoice series.** Keep separate, consecutive series.
- **Leaving out value on the challan.** Even without tax, the value is needed and drives the e-way bill.

## Which one do I need? A quick check

1. Is ownership passing for a price right now? → **Tax invoice**
2. Are goods going for job work, on approval, for exhibition, or to your own place within the same GSTIN? → **Delivery challan**
3. Is the exact quantity unknown at dispatch (liquid gas, line sales)? → **Delivery challan**, invoice later
4. Is it an inter-state transfer to your own branch with a different GSTIN? → Usually **tax invoice**; confirm
