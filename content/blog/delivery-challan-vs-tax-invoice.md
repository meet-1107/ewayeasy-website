> **Quick answer:** Issue a tax invoice when a taxable supply takes place. Issue a delivery challan when goods move without a sale, or the sale is not complete at removal, such as job work, supply on approval or a same-GSTIN stock transfer. Where an e-way bill is needed, generate it against whichever document applies.

Goods leave your premises for many reasons. You may be selling them, or sending them to a job worker, a customer on approval, your own godown or a sales van. GST treats each differently, so the document must match what is actually happening.

## Tax invoice or delivery challan: the core difference

A tax invoice is the document for a taxable supply: ownership passes for a price, tax is charged, and the buyer can claim credit. A delivery challan is a movement document for goods that leave without a completed sale, or where the invoice cannot be raised at removal. Both carry value, and both can support an e-way bill.

The legal basis in brief:

- **Tax invoice:** Section 31 of the CGST Act and Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, 2017.
- **Delivery challan:** Rule 55 of the CGST Rules, 2017.

Rule 55 allows a challan in place of an invoice at removal in specified cases:

- Supply of liquid gas where the quantity at removal is not known
- Transportation of goods for job work
- Transportation of goods for reasons other than by way of supply
- Other supplies as may be notified (such as goods sent on approval)

It also covers goods moved in semi-knocked down (SKD) or completely knocked down (CKD) condition in several lots. There, the full invoice comes before the first lot and each lot moves with a challan.

## Side-by-side comparison

The comparison comes down to five points: purpose, tax, credit, typical cases and copies. An invoice charges tax and supports the buyer's credit, while a challan records movement, carries no tax liability of its own and gives no credit. Both need their own consecutive number series, unique for the financial year.

| Point | Tax invoice | Delivery challan |
|---|---|---|
| Used when | A taxable supply of goods takes place | Goods move without a sale, or the sale is not complete at removal |
| Tax charged? | Yes, shown on the document | No tax liability arises from the challan itself |
| Basis for ITC | Yes, for the recipient | No |
| Typical cases | Normal sales, inter-state sales, exports | Job work, approval sales, line sales, same-GSTIN transfers, exhibitions, SKD/CKD lots |
| Copies (goods) | Original for recipient; duplicate for transporter; triplicate for supplier | Original for consignee; duplicate for transporter; triplicate for consignor |
| Serial number | Consecutive series, unique for the financial year | Consecutive series, unique for the financial year |
| E-way bill basis? | Yes | Yes |

The stock transfer test is about GSTINs, not states. A branch in another state always has a different GSTIN, so it is a distinct person and usually needs a tax invoice. A separate registration in the same state is also a distinct person, so only a place of business under your own GSTIN fits the plain challan route.

| If the goods go to… | Then the document is usually… |
|---|---|
| Your godown under the same GSTIN | Delivery challan |
| Your own separate registration in the same state | Tax invoice (distinct persons) |
| Your branch in another state | Tax invoice (distinct persons) |

Confirm the treatment for your own structure with your adviser.

## What a delivery challan should contain

A delivery challan under Rule 55 must show its date and number, the consignor's and consignee's details, HSN and description, quantity, taxable value, tax where the movement is a supply, place of supply for inter-state movement, and a signature. Missing value is the most common gap, because people assume no tax means no value.

In practical terms, your challan should have:

- Date and number (consecutive, unique series not exceeding 16 characters)
- Name, address and GSTIN of the consignor
- Name, address and GSTIN (or UIN) of the consignee, if registered
- HSN code and description of goods
- Quantity (provisional where exact quantity is not known)
- Taxable value
- Tax rate and tax amount, where the transportation is for supply to the consignee
- Place of supply, in the case of inter-state movement
- Signature

Two small habits save trouble at the receiving end. Print the purpose on the challan, such as "Sent for job work" or "Not a tax invoice". And keep the challan number within the same character set you use for invoices (letters, numbers, hyphen, slash), so it goes into every system without edits.

## Example (illustrative): job work and final sale

This worked example follows one batch of goods through three movements. Yarn goes to a job worker on a challan, fabric comes back on a challan, and the finished fabric is sold on a tax invoice. Each movement gets the document that matches what is happening, and each gets its own e-way bill.

A Ludhiana hosiery unit sends 2,000 kg of yarn to a job worker in Tiruppur for knitting. Later, it sells the finished fabric to a garment exporter in Mumbai.

**Movement 1: yarn to the job worker (Punjab → Tamil Nadu)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Document | Delivery challan DC/26/041 |
| Purpose | Sent for job work |
| Value of yarn | ₹4,60,000 |
| Tax on document | Not charged as a supply |
| E-way bill | Required. Inter-state movement from principal to job worker needs one irrespective of value |

**Movement 2: fabric back from the job worker**

The job worker returns the knitted fabric to Ludhiana under a challan. An e-way bill is needed where the conditions apply; see [e-way bill rules for job work](/blog/eway-bill-rules-for-job-work) for who generates it.

**Movement 3: sale to the Mumbai exporter (Punjab → Maharashtra)**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fabric, 1,750 kg × ₹380 | ₹6,65,000 |
| IGST @ 5% (illustrative rate) | ₹33,250 |
| **Invoice total** | **₹6,98,250** |

This is a supply, so a tax invoice with IGST is issued, and the e-way bill is generated against it. Three movements, two document types, three e-way bills.

## Supply on approval and line sales

Supply on approval and line sales both use a challan because the sale is not complete when goods leave. On approval, the customer has not yet decided to buy; in line sales, you do not know which shop will buy what. In both cases, the tax invoice follows when each sale actually happens.

**Goods sent on approval:** a jeweller or machinery dealer sends goods for the customer to inspect first. A challan covers the movement, and a tax invoice follows if the customer accepts. GST law sets a time limit for issuing that invoice, so check the current provision.

**Line sales or a sales van:** the quantity for each customer is not known at dispatch. Goods move under a challan, and invoices are raised as each sale is made on the route.

The practical trap here is the open challan, so keep a register of approval challans with a "due by" date and match each one to an invoice or a return. An unmatched challan months later is hard to explain. For returns and other special cases, see [e-way bills for returns and special transactions](/blog/eway-bill-returns-and-special-transactions).

## Common mistakes

The common mistakes fall into two groups: using the wrong document, and treating the challan as less serious than an invoice. A challan used to delay tax on a real sale is wrong, and so is an invoice for job work. Leaving value off a challan, or skipping its e-way bill, causes trouble in transit.

- **Using a challan for an actual sale** to delay tax. If ownership passes and consideration is agreed, it is a supply and needs an invoice.
- **Using an invoice for job work.** Sending goods to a job worker is not a sale; a GST invoice here confuses both parties' books.
- **Forgetting the e-way bill for job work.** Inter-state movement to a job worker needs one irrespective of value.
- **Not converting approval challans into invoices** within the time limit.
- **Mixing challan and invoice series.** Keep separate, consecutive series.
- **Leaving out value on the challan.** Even without tax, the value is needed and drives the e-way bill.

## Which one do I need? A quick check

The fastest way to choose is to ask four questions in order: is ownership passing for a price now, are goods going for job work, approval, exhibition or to your own place under the same GSTIN, is the quantity unknown at dispatch, and is the receiver your own separate registration? The first "yes" decides the document.

1. Is ownership passing for a price right now? → **Tax invoice**
2. Are goods going for job work, on approval, for exhibition, or to your own place under the same GSTIN? → **Delivery challan**
3. Is the exact quantity unknown at dispatch (liquid gas, line sales)? → **Delivery challan**, invoice later
4. Is it a transfer to your own branch with a different GSTIN? → Usually **tax invoice**; confirm

## Quick action checklist

1. Set up a separate, consecutive challan series for this financial year.
2. Add a "purpose" line to your challan format, such as job work, approval or own use.
3. Start a register of open approval and job work challans with a due date for each.
4. List your own registrations and godowns, and mark which ones need an invoice instead of a challan.
5. Check every challan has a value before the goods leave.
