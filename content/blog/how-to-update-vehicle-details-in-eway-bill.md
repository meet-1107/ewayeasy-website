An e-way bill is not a document you can freely edit. Once it is generated, the Part A details — GSTINs, invoice number, value, HSN — are locked. The one section that is designed to change during the journey is **Part B**, which holds the vehicle number or transport document details. If your truck breaks down on the highway, or goods are moved from a small tempo to a long-haul trailer at a hub, Part B is what you update.

This guide explains when a vehicle update is needed, who is allowed to do it, how the process works on the official portal, and the mistakes that typically get a vehicle stopped at a checkpoint.

## Why the vehicle number matters

Under Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, 2017, an e-way bill is valid for movement only after Part B is filled (the limited exception is short intra-state movement of up to 50 km to or from a transporter's place, where Part A alone is enough for that leg). During an inspection, the officer compares the vehicle physically carrying the goods with the vehicle number shown in the e-way bill. If they do not match, the officer may treat the goods as moving without a valid e-way bill — and that is where detention and penalty under Section 129 of the CGST Act come into the picture.

In simple terms: **the vehicle carrying the goods at any moment must be the vehicle shown in Part B at that moment.**

## When you need to update Part B

| Situation | Update needed? |
|---|---|
| Truck breaks down and goods are loaded onto another truck | Yes, before the new vehicle moves |
| Goods unloaded at a transporter's godown and reloaded onto a different vehicle (trans-shipment) | Yes |
| Part B was left blank at generation because the vehicle was not yet assigned | Yes, before movement starts |
| Transporter changes the driver but the vehicle stays the same | No |
| Goods move by rail, air or ship and a new transport document is issued | Update transport document details as applicable |
| Wrong vehicle number entered at generation | Yes — update Part B with the correct number |

> **Note:** A wrong *Part A* detail (wrong GSTIN, invoice number or value) cannot be fixed through a Part B update. That needs cancellation within 24 hours and a fresh e-way bill. See our guide on [e-way bill correction](/eway-bill-correction).

## Who can update the vehicle details

The person who generated the e-way bill can update Part B. If the generator has assigned the e-way bill to a transporter (by entering the transporter's GSTIN or Transporter ID), the transporter can update it too. In multi-leg journeys, the e-way bill can be handed from one transporter to the next, and the transporter currently in charge updates Part B.

Unregistered transporters can enrol on the e-way bill portal to obtain a Transporter ID (TRANSIN) so that consignors can assign e-way bills to them.

## Step-by-step: updating vehicle details on the portal

The exact screens change from time to time, so treat this as a general walkthrough and check the official user documentation for the current layout.

1. **Log in** to the e-way bill portal (or the E-Way Bill 2.0 portal) with your user ID, password and the multi-factor authentication now required for taxpayers.
2. Go to the **e-way bill menu** and choose the option to update Part B / vehicle number.
3. **Search the e-way bill** by its number (or by generation date/document details where available).
4. Select the **mode of transport** — road, rail, air or ship.
5. Enter the **new vehicle number** (for road) or the **transport document number and date** (for rail/air/ship).
6. Enter the **place and state from where the goods are now starting** — usually the place where the breakdown or trans-shipment happened.
7. Choose the **reason** for the update from the dropdown (for example breakdown, trans-shipment or first-time entry) and add remarks if needed.
8. **Submit** and download or note the updated e-way bill. The vehicle details history on the e-way bill will show every change.

The portal also provides an option for situations where one consignment has to be split across more than one vehicle. If you face that case, refer to the multi-vehicle section of the official user documentation rather than improvising.

## Example (illustrative): a breakdown near Udaipur

A Rajkot pipe manufacturer sends PVC pipes worth ₹3,40,000 (including GST) to a dealer in Jaipur. The e-way bill is generated at 10:00 am on 12 June with vehicle GJ03AB1234 in Part B. Distance is 620 km, so validity is 4 days (1 day per 200 km or part thereof: 620 ÷ 200 = 3.1, rounded up to 4).

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Generated | 12 June, 10:00 am |
| Distance | 620 km |
| Validity | 4 days — up to midnight of 16 June |
| Original vehicle | GJ03AB1234 |

On 13 June the truck's clutch fails near Udaipur. The transporter arranges another truck, RJ27CD5678. Before the goods leave Udaipur in the new truck, the transporter logs in, updates Part B with RJ27CD5678, enters Udaipur (Rajasthan) as the place of change, and selects "breakdown" as the reason.

What does **not** change: the e-way bill number, the invoice details and the validity. The goods still need to reach Jaipur by midnight of 16 June. If the breakdown causes a long delay, the transporter should watch the clock and use the extension facility if needed — see [what to do when an e-way bill expires](/blog/what-to-do-when-eway-bill-expires).

## Common mistakes

- **Moving first, updating later.** The update should be done before the new vehicle starts moving with the goods. A checkpoint between the breakdown spot and the next update is exactly where problems happen.
- **Typing errors in the vehicle number.** Spaces, the letter O instead of zero, or a missing district code all cause a mismatch. Read the number off the registration plate, not from memory.
- **Wrong "from" place.** Entering the original dispatch city instead of the place where the vehicle changed makes the record inconsistent with the actual route.
- **Trying to fix Part A through Part B.** If the invoice value or recipient GSTIN is wrong, a vehicle update will not help.
- **Assuming the update extends validity.** It does not. Extension is a separate process with its own time window.
- **Not passing the e-way bill to the next transporter.** In a multi-transporter chain, if the e-way bill is not assigned onward, the new transporter cannot update Part B.

## Limitations and exceptions to keep in mind

- **Expired e-way bills** cannot be updated. Once validity is over, you are in extension territory (within 8 hours before or after expiry, subject to the overall 360-day cap reported by GSTN from 1 January 2025).
- **Consolidated e-way bills (EWB-02)** carry their own vehicle details. If you move several consignments together in one vehicle under a consolidated e-way bill and the vehicle changes, update the consolidated e-way bill as per the portal's process.
- **Short-distance movements** within a State/UT up to 50 km to or from a transporter's place may not need Part B for that leg, but Part B must be filled before the onward journey.
