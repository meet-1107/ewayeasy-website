Wholesalers and distributors face a different e-way bill problem from manufacturers. A factory usually sends one large consignment to one buyer. A distributor sends one vehicle out with ten or fifteen invoices, some above the threshold and some below, to retailers spread across a route. Add godown transfers, returns from retailers and goods that are billed to one party and delivered to another, and the paperwork gets messy fast.

This guide covers the situations distributors run into most often and how to handle each one cleanly.

## The basic rule, briefly

Under Section 68 of the CGST Act and Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, an e-way bill is generally required before goods move when the consignment value exceeds ₹50,000. Consignment value means the value declared in the invoice, bill of supply or delivery challan, including GST, but excluding the value of exempt supplies in the same document.

For intra-state movement, some states have notified different thresholds or exemptions. Check your state's notification on its commercial tax website or the official e-way bill portal. For more on this, see [the ₹50,000 threshold and its exceptions](/blog/eway-bill-limit-50000-threshold-exceptions).

## Situation 1: Many invoices, one vehicle

This is the everyday distributor scenario. The practical approach:

1. **Identify which invoices cross the threshold.** Each invoice (consignment) is generally assessed on its own value.
2. **Generate an e-way bill for each qualifying invoice.** Part A for each one comes from the invoice. Part B carries the same vehicle number.
3. **Consider a consolidated e-way bill (EWB-02).** The transporter, or you if you run your own vehicle, can group the individual e-way bills into one consolidated document for the trip. The driver then carries one consolidated reference instead of a stack.
4. **Carry the invoices for all goods**, including those below the threshold. An officer can still ask for documents for every consignment on the vehicle.

> Note: Do not split one supply into several invoices just to keep each below the limit. That defeats the purpose of the rule and can be questioned during inspection.

### Validity on multi-drop routes

Validity depends on distance: one day for every 200 km or part of it for normal cargo, with the first day ending at midnight of the day after generation. Each e-way bill has its own distance, based on its own PIN-to-PIN route. On a long multi-drop route, the last drop's e-way bill will need the longest validity, so enter its actual distance correctly. See [how distance affects e-way bill validity](/blog/how-distance-affects-eway-bill-validity).

## Situation 2: Godown-to-godown and godown-to-shop transfers

Distributors often move stock between their own places of business.

| Transfer type | Typical document | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Same GSTIN, same state (e.g. godown to shop) | Delivery challan | Not a supply; e-way bill still needed if value crosses the threshold applicable in your state |
| Different GSTINs of the same business, different states | Tax invoice | Treated as a supply between distinct persons; IGST applies; e-way bill needed above ₹50,000 |

For the same-GSTIN case, the reason for transport on the portal is usually "own use" or a similar option; for the inter-state case, it is a supply. Value the delivery challan properly — an understated value can attract questions.

## Situation 3: Bill to one party, ship to another

A common distributor arrangement: you bill a retailer in one town, but the goods go directly from your supplier's warehouse, or you bill a head office and deliver to its branch. The e-way bill portal provides transaction-type options for such "bill-to / ship-to" and "bill-from / dispatch-from" cases. The key is that Part A reflects the actual place of delivery (the ship-to PIN), because that is what determines the route and distance. Read [E-way bill rules for returns and special transactions](/blog/eway-bill-returns-and-special-transactions) for more on these arrangements.

## Situation 4: Returns from retailers

When a retailer sends back damaged or unsold stock, goods are moving again and the threshold applies to that movement. Who generates the e-way bill depends on who causes the movement — often the retailer (if registered) or the transporter. Distributors should agree in advance with retailers how returns will be documented, so goods do not get stuck on the road.

## Situation 5: Vehicle breakdown or change mid-route

If the vehicle breaks down or the load is transferred to another vehicle, update Part B with the new vehicle number before the goods move further. Part B can be updated any number of times during validity. If you used a consolidated e-way bill, it can be updated for the new vehicle too. See [how to update vehicle details in an e-way bill](/blog/how-to-update-vehicle-details-in-eway-bill).

## Example (illustrative)

A Surat textile distributor (GSTIN `24ABCDE1234F1Z5`) loads a tempo with goods for six retailers in Bharuch and Vadodara:

| Retailer | Invoice value incl. GST | E-way bill? |
|---|---|---|
| A | ₹18,500 | Generally not required |
| B | ₹72,000 | Required |
| C | ₹41,000 | Generally not required |
| D | ₹1,15,000 | Required |
| E | ₹56,300 | Required |
| F | ₹9,800 | Generally not required |

The distributor:

1. Generates three e-way bills (B, D, E), each with its own delivery PIN and distance.
2. Fills Part B with the tempo number on all three.
3. Generates a consolidated e-way bill for the trip, since the distributor runs its own vehicle.
4. Hands the driver all six invoices plus the consolidated e-way bill reference.

Midway, the tempo develops a fault and goods are shifted to a second vehicle. The distributor updates the vehicle number on the consolidated e-way bill before the new vehicle leaves.

## Common mistakes distributors make

- **Assuming the vehicle total, not the invoice value, decides everything.** The threshold is generally per consignment. Check your state's rules if you are unsure.
- **Wrong delivery PIN.** Using your own godown PIN or the billing PIN instead of the actual delivery PIN gives a wrong distance and validity.
- **Forgetting the low-value invoices.** They still need invoices on board.
- **No e-way bill for stock transfers.** Moving stock to your own shop is not a sale, but the e-way bill rule can still apply.
- **Late Part B updates.** If goods move in a vehicle that is not in Part B, that can be treated as moving without a valid e-way bill.
- **Copying the buyer name from the order instead of the GST registration.** Small spelling differences create confusion during verification.

## How to make this routine

- Keep a saved list of retailers with verified GSTIN and delivery PIN.
- Sort the day's invoices by value before loading, so you know which need e-way bills.
- Assign one person to generate e-way bills and one to check vehicle numbers.
- Keep a simple daily log: invoice number, e-way bill number, vehicle number, drop status.
