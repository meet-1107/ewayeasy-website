> **Quick answer:** You charge GST on an invoice only if you are GST-registered and the supply is taxable; that document is a tax invoice. If you are registered but the supply is exempt, or you are under the composition scheme, you issue a bill of supply with no GST on it. If you are not registered at all, you issue a plain bill and cannot charge GST.

"With GST or without GST?" sounds like a choice you make for each customer. It is not. Whether GST appears on your document depends on two facts about you and the item you are selling: are you registered, and is this supply taxable? Once those are clear, the type of document follows automatically.

This guide is about that decision. For the full list of mandatory fields, copies and time limits, see our [GST invoice rules guide](/gst-invoice-rules). For working out the tax amount itself, see [how to calculate GST on a sales invoice](/blog/how-to-calculate-gst-on-sales-invoice).

## The three situations

### 1. Registered seller, taxable supply: tax invoice with GST

If you hold a GSTIN as a regular taxpayer and you sell goods or services that attract GST, you must issue a tax invoice (Section 31 of the CGST Act, Rule 46 of the CGST Rules) and charge GST on it. You cannot skip GST because the buyer asks for a "kachha bill" or because the customer is unregistered. The buyer's status changes what you print (their GSTIN or not, place of supply), not whether you charge tax.

Within your state you charge CGST + SGST; to another state, IGST. Our explainer on [CGST, SGST and IGST](/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-explained) covers how to pick the right head.

### 2. Registered seller, no GST charged: bill of supply

A registered person issues a **bill of supply** instead of a tax invoice in two main cases (Section 31(3)(c) and Rule 49):

- **Exempt supplies.** For example, a trader in a registered business selling an item that is exempt from GST. No tax is charged, so a tax invoice would be the wrong document.
- **Composition dealers.** A business that has opted for the composition scheme pays tax at a fixed rate on turnover from its own pocket. It is not allowed to collect GST from customers, so every sale goes on a bill of supply. The bill must carry the words **"composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"** at the top.

### 3. Unregistered seller: plain bill, no GST

If your turnover is below the registration threshold and you have not taken voluntary registration, you are outside the GST invoicing system. You can and should still give the customer a bill or cash memo showing what was sold and the price, but **it must not show GST and must not show a GSTIN** (you do not have one). Bill of supply rules under Rule 49 are written for registered persons; for an unregistered seller, a simple, clear bill is the practical norm.

## What changes on the document

| Field | Tax invoice (with GST) | Bill of supply (registered, no GST) | Plain bill (unregistered) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Document title | "Tax Invoice" | "Bill of Supply" | "Bill", "Invoice" or "Cash Memo" |
| Your GSTIN | Required | Required | None (you have no GSTIN) |
| Buyer's GSTIN | If buyer is registered | If buyer is registered | Not needed |
| HSN / SAC code | Required as per your turnover rules | Required as per your turnover rules | Not required under GST |
| Taxable value | Shown | Value of supply shown | Price shown |
| CGST / SGST / IGST columns | Rate and amount shown | Not shown | Not shown |
| Place of supply | Required for inter-state supply | Not a tax-charging field | Not needed |
| Special declaration | Reverse charge statement where applicable | Composition dealers: "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" | None |
| Buyer can claim ITC? | Yes, if other conditions are met | No | No |

The detailed field-by-field requirements, including HSN digit rules, are in the [GST invoice rules guide](/gst-invoice-rules).

## Invoice-cum-bill of supply (Rule 46A)

Some registered businesses sell a mix of taxable and exempt items in one transaction. A general store selling packaged goods alongside an exempt item is a common example. Strictly, that would need a tax invoice for the taxable items and a bill of supply for the exempt ones.

Rule 46A gives a simpler route: where a registered person supplies **both taxable and exempt** goods or services **to an unregistered person**, a single **invoice-cum-bill of supply** can be issued for all of them. The taxable lines carry GST; the exempt lines show no tax. If the buyer is registered, issue the documents separately as usual.

## Example (illustrative): three sellers, three documents

Three businesses in Gujarat each sell goods worth ₹20,000 to the same buyer, a Rajkot pipe manufacturer with sample GSTIN `24ABCDE1234F1Z5`.

| Seller | Status | Document issued | GST on document | Total payable by buyer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Surat textile trader | Regular registered, taxable goods at an illustrative 5% | Tax invoice | CGST ₹500 + SGST ₹500 | ₹21,000 |
| A Morbi hardware shop | Composition dealer | Bill of supply with composition declaration | None | ₹20,000 |
| A small Jamnagar workshop | Not registered | Plain bill | None | ₹20,000 |

Only the first document lets the buyer claim ₹1,000 as input tax credit. The composition dealer still pays its own composition tax to the government, but cannot pass it on as GST. The unregistered workshop pays no GST on this sale at all.

*Rates are for illustration only. Confirm the current rate for your goods before invoicing.*

## Decision flow: which document do I issue?

- **If** you are not registered under GST → **then** issue a plain bill. Do not charge GST or print a GSTIN.
- **If** you are registered under the composition scheme → **then** issue a bill of supply with the composition declaration and no tax.
- **If** you are a regular registered person and the item is exempt → **then** issue a bill of supply.
- **If** you are a regular registered person and the item is taxable → **then** issue a tax invoice with GST, whatever the buyer's status.
- **If** one sale has both taxable and exempt items and the buyer is unregistered → **then** you may issue an invoice-cum-bill of supply under Rule 46A.
- **If** you are unsure whether an item is exempt → **then** check the HSN and rate notification on the official portal before choosing the format.

## Common mistakes

- **Charging GST without registration.** An unregistered seller who adds "GST 18%" to a bill is collecting tax it has no right to collect. It also misleads the buyer, who cannot claim that amount as credit. If your sales have crossed the threshold, register first.
- **Composition dealer showing tax.** Printing CGST/SGST on a composition dealer's bill is a common error, often because of a default template. Composition dealers must not collect tax on supplies.
- **Registered seller dropping GST "on request".** A regular registered seller cannot issue a bill without GST for a taxable item because the customer wants a lower price. The tax is still due.
- **Using a tax invoice for exempt items.** Showing "GST 0%" on a tax invoice for exempt goods is better replaced by a bill of supply.
- **Missing declarations.** A bill of supply from a composition dealer without the required words is incomplete.
- **Mixing series.** Keep your tax invoices and bills of supply in consecutive series that are unique for the financial year. Our [invoice number generator](/tools/invoice-number-generator) helps you set a clean format.

## Before you issue either document

- If the buyer is registered, check their GSTIN with the [GSTIN validator](/tools/gstin-validator). A wrong GSTIN on a tax invoice affects their credit.
- If you are charging GST, check the amount with the [GST calculator](/tools/gst-calculator).
- If goods are moving and the consignment value crosses the e-way bill threshold, the e-way bill is generated against the tax invoice or bill of supply. See [e-way bill vs sales invoice](/eway-bill-vs-sales-invoice).
